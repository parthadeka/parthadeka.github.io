Teaching & mentorship

A fourteen-lecture course in radio astronomy, taught to undergraduates at the University of Cape Town

Radio astronomy is usually met for the first time as a wall of unfamiliar machinery. Across fourteen lectures the course takes students from the radio sky and the single dish through interferometry and calibration to spectral lines and modern surveys, with the mathematics introduced only where it earns its place.

01 / Course structure
14 lectures · University of Cape Town · undergraduate level
Block I
Lectures 1–3

The radio sky and the single dish

Radiative transfer and brightness temperature; thermal and non-thermal emission; antenna beams, gain and system temperature; what a single dish can and cannot resolve.

Block II
Lectures 4–7

Interferometry from first principles

The two-element interferometer; visibilities and the uv plane; the van Cittert–Zernike relation; aperture synthesis, sampling and the dirty beam.

Block III
Lectures 8–11

Calibration, imaging and deconvolution

Flagging and RFI; bandpass, gain and flux-scale calibration; CLEAN and its variants; self-calibration; how imaging artefacts are recognised and where they come from.

Block IV
Lectures 12–14

Spectral lines, surveys and science

H I 21-cm emission and absorption; spectral-line cubes and continuum subtraction; source finding and catalogues; radio surveys with MeerKAT and towards the SKA.

02 / Supervision
Co-supervisor, M.Sc.
2026 — present

Detection and characterisation of radio sources in MeerLIRGs, and the search for anomalous radio sources

University of Cape Town. The project builds a reliable source catalogue for the MeerLIRGs sample and then looks for the rare and morphologically unusual — bent jets, extended structure, sources whose radio and infrared properties disagree.
