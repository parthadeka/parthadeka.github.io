Search for neutral H I 21-cm absorption associated with low- and high-excitation radio-loud AGNs from MALS

P. P. Deka, N. Gupta, and the MALS collaboration
Accepted for publication in A&A
In plain words — summary to follow on publication

Radio-loud galaxies come in two broad kinds: those whose surroundings are strongly excited by the central black hole, and those whose surroundings are not. This paper searches both kinds, using MeerKAT, for the shadow that cold hydrogen gas casts on the galaxy's own radio emission.

A&A 687, A50

MALS discovery of a rare H I 21-cm absorber at z ∼ 1.35: origin of the absorbing gas in powerful active galactic nuclei

P. P. Deka, N. Gupta, H.-W. Chen, S. D. Johnson, P. Noterdaeme, F. Combes, E. Boettcher, S. A. Balashev, K. L. Emig, G. I. G. Józsa, H.-R. Klöckner, J.-K. Krogager, E. Momjian, P. Petitjean, G. C. Rudie, J. Wagenveld, F. S. Zahedy
In plain words

We detected cold hydrogen gas silhouetted against the quasar J2339−5523, whose light left it around nine billion years ago — only six such detections were known in this era of the universe. The gas sits directly in front of the quasar's core, so it must lie very close to the black hole itself. Comparing this system with 16 quasars and 19 radio galaxies of matched radio brightness, a consistent picture emerges: in quasars the absorbing gas is mostly in a small disc around the nucleus and in the shell of material swept up by the radio jet, whereas in radio galaxies it comes from that shell plus gas spread across the whole galaxy. Which type of galaxy you look at therefore determines which part of its gas reservoir you happen to see.

The MeerKAT Absorption Line Survey (MALS) Data Release I: Stokes I image catalogs at 1–1.4 GHz

P. P. Deka, N. Gupta, P. Jagannathan, S. Sekhar, E. Momjian, S. Bhatnagar, J. Wagenveld, H.-R. Klöckner, J. Jose, S. A. Balashev, F. Combes, M. Hilton, D. Borgaonkar, A. Chatterjee, K. L. Emig, A. N. Gaunekar, G. I. G. Józsa, D. Y. Klutse, K. Knowles, J.-K. Krogager, A. Mohapatra, K. Moodley, S. Muller, P. Noterdaeme, P. Petitjean, P. Salas, S. Sikhosana
In plain words

MeerKAT observed 391 patches of sky, and this paper turns those observations into a public catalogue of 495,325 radio sources covering 2,289 square degrees. Everything was processed the same way, so the images are directly comparable to one another; we then measured how good they are, establishing positions accurate to about one arcsecond and brightnesses to within a few per cent by comparison with earlier surveys. Along the way we identified 1,308 sources that have changed in brightness and 122 that have appeared or vanished over the past 26 years, most of them active galaxies. The images and catalogues are freely available at mals.iucaa.in.

JApA 42, 46

A pair of UV nuclei or a compact star-forming region near the active nucleus in Mrk 766?

P. P. Deka, G. C. Dewangan, K. P. Singh, J. Postma
In plain words

Observing the nearby active galaxy Mrk 766 in ultraviolet light with India's AstroSat satellite, we found a second compact bright spot about 1.1 kiloparsecs from the known nucleus — so the galaxy appears to have a pair of ultraviolet nuclei. The new spot is a few times fainter than the nucleus itself, which raised the possibility of a second feeding black hole left over from a galaxy merger. But it emits no X-rays, and its colours across four ultraviolet and optical bands match those of a starburst rather than a black hole. It is therefore almost certainly a dense region of newly forming stars, not a second nucleus.
